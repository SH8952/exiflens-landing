sRGB vs Adobe RGB: which one should you use?

Updated: 2026-11-15

Short answer

For almost everything that will be viewed on a phone, website or social feed, use sRGB. Adobe RGB holds more saturated greens and cyans, but it only helps if your whole chain (camera, editor, monitor, printer) is color-managed. Otherwise the photo can look flat and dull.

The short comparison

sRGBAdobe RGB (1998)
GamutSmaller; matches most screensWider, especially in greens and cyans (commonly cited as about 35% larger)
Best forWeb, social media, phones, email, most printing servicesPrint workflows and wide-gamut monitors
RiskVery lowLooks muted in apps that ignore color profiles

Why Adobe RGB can look dull online

An Adobe RGB file stores bigger color values. If a browser or app does not read the embedded profile, it treats those values as sRGB and shows them with less saturation. Most phones and many websites handle profiles correctly, but you cannot control every viewer, so sRGB is the safe default for sharing.

Does the camera setting matter if you shoot RAW?

No. In RAW mode the color space setting does not change the sensor data; it mostly affects the JPEG preview and the tag saved in the file. You choose the real color space when you export from your RAW editor. If you shoot JPEG only, the camera setting is the one that counts, and sRGB is the safer choice.

Simple checklist

  • Posting online or sending to clients? Export in sRGB.
  • Printing at home or at a lab that accepts Adobe RGB and you have a wide-gamut monitor? Adobe RGB can help.
  • Not sure? Export sRGB. You will rarely regret it.
  • Always embed the color profile when you export.

FAQ

Should I shoot in Adobe RGB to be safe?

Not usually. If you shoot RAW, the setting changes little. If you shoot JPEG, Adobe RGB files can look dull when shared unless you convert them to sRGB first.

Do I need an Adobe RGB monitor?

Only if you work with print or professional color workflows. A standard sRGB monitor shows sRGB photos correctly and is fine for most photographers.

Can I convert Adobe RGB to sRGB later?

Yes. Convert with your editor's export settings. The conversion clips colors that sRGB cannot show, so some very saturated areas will look slightly different.
